At the naka, a roadside hiring spot, contractors pick daily wage workers by face and pay them in cash. Nothing is written down, so a good worker has no way to prove it.

Four people meet at the naka every morning.

Drawn from our interviews: two workers, the contractor who hires them, and the foreman in between.

S

Sunita, 38Worker · daily wage, migrant

“Roz uthke pata nahi hota kaam milega ya nahi. Aise life kaise plan kare?”

Every morning I don’t know if I’ll get work. How do you plan a life like that?

Paid less than the men, and has been cut and not paid at all.

Ramesh, 42Worker · shuttering, semi-skilled

“11 saal kaam kiya hai, par aaj bhi koi guarantee nahi hai ki kal kaam milega.”

I’ve worked 11 years, and there’s still no guarantee I’ll get work tomorrow.

Treated as new by every contractor who doesn’t know him.

Shailesh, 48Contractor · residential projects

“Kaun milega aaj, pata hi nahi hota. Planning karna mushkil hai.”

I never know who I’ll get today. Planning is hard.

Loses every morning to finding workers he can trust.

Mustafa, 35Foreman · the middleman

“Dono taraf ka kaam mere through hi hota hai… par jab kuch galat hota hai toh blame bhi mujhe hi milta hai.”

Both sides’ work goes through me… but when something goes wrong, I’m the one who’s blamed.

All the responsibility, none of the authority.

The schemes meant to help don’t reach them.

The government runs welfare schemes for informal workers, from e-Shram registration to accident insurance. But it isn’t linked to the daily work, and signing up takes help most workers don’t have.

  • 30 cr+workers registered on e-Shram, but inactive
  • 60%unaware of the schemes, or confused by them
  • 90%get no direct or usable benefit

One job, end to end.

The service blueprint follows the worker from the night before to project end, with what the coordinator, the contractor and the system do at every stage, above and below the lines of interaction and visibility.

ULS gives every worker a record.

ULS adds a service layer to the naka without replacing it: a QR-coded ID card that carries a worker’s skills, work and pay, and three simple screens built around it, for the worker, the foreman and the contractor.

This page is the short version.Full case study

The card needs no smartphone. A coordinator at the naka scans it, signs new workers up, and links their e-Shram and insurance. Workers without a phone get their job details by SMS.

The worker’s app and the foreman’s console

The worker knows where to report, when, and for what wage, before leaving home. The foreman logs attendance and pay as they happen, in Hindi first. Mark Prakash present, or switch the language.
